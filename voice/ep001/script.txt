You're accused of a crime in medieval England. Three ways to prove you're innocent. Only one actually works.
Option one: the hot iron. Carry it three steps, then a priest bandages your hand.
Three days later, healing means innocent. Infection means guilty.
Milo's hand? Not healing. Guilty.
Fine. Option two: trial by combat.
The Normans brought it to England after 1066. Whoever wins is innocent.
You can hire a champion. Milo hired his cousin Greg. Your accuser hired a giant.
Greg lasts four seconds.
Two down. Milo's out of options. And the last one sounds ridiculous.
Option three: read one psalm out loud. That's it.
Church courts went easier on churchmen, and by the 1300s, judges let ordinary people claim the same deal if they could read.
Memorize Psalm 51, and you could dodge the noose. They called it the neck verse.
Milo opens the book... and starts to read.
Verdict: innocent.
The best defense in medieval England wasn't strength. It was knowing how to read.
So which would you pick? One, two, or three?
